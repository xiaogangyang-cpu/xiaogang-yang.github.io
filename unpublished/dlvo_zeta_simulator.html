▶ What is Zeta Potential? ▸

When a charged particle is immersed in an electrolyte, counter-ions condense near the surface forming the Stern layer (inner compact region). Beyond this, the diffuse layer extends into the bulk.

The slip plane (shear plane) separates ions that move with the particle from the bulk solution. The electric potential at this plane is the Zeta potential (ζ) — experimentally measurable via electrophoresis.

ψ(x) = ψ₀ · exp(−κx)

ψ₀ = surface potential, κ⁻¹ = Debye screening length, measured in nm.

▶ What is Debye Length? ▸

The Debye length (κ⁻¹) characterizes how far electrostatic interactions extend from a charged surface into solution. High ionic strength compresses the double layer, reducing κ⁻¹.

κ⁻¹ = √(εᵣε₀k_BT / 2N_Ae²I)

At 1 mM NaCl: κ⁻¹ ≈ 9.6 nm. At 100 mM: κ⁻¹ ≈ 0.96 nm. Divalent ions compress the double layer 4× more than monovalent at the same concentration (Schulze-Hardy rule).

▶ What is DLVO Theory? ▸

DLVO theory (Derjaguin, Landau, Verwey, Overbeek) describes colloidal stability as a competition between:

• Van der Waals attraction (short range) — always present, drives aggregation

• Electrostatic repulsion (double-layer) — depends on surface charge and ionic strength

V_total = V_EDL + V_vdW

An energy barrier >25 kT prevents particles from reaching primary contact. Below 10 kT, Brownian motion causes irreversible aggregation (coagulation).
